THE PROBLEM

Basements Face Pressure, Not Just Moisture.

Water Under Pressure Is a Different Problem Entirely

A terrace leaks because rain gets in. A basement floods because groundwater is actively pushed through concrete by hydrostatic pressure. Surface treatments fail immediately. You need a system rated for pressure.

Why Basements Fail

  • Construction joint failure — The joint between base slab and walls is the primary entry point in 70% of basement failures.
  • Hydrostatic pressure — Monsoon raises the water table. Saturated soil pushes against the wall. Pressure forces water through any weakness.
  • Rebar corrosion — Water reaches reinforcement, corrodes it, expands. Expanding rust spalls concrete from the inside.
  • Wrong product choice — Damp-proof paint (bitumen or acrylic) has zero pressure resistance. It peels off as soon as hydrostatic pressure develops.
THE SOLUTION

Two Systems: Interior or Exterior Tanking.

Interior tanking stops water from entering the habitable space. Exterior tanking stops water before it contacts the structure. Choose based on access and stage of construction.

APPLICATION TIMELINE

7-Day Interior Tanking Process.

Day 1

Active Leak Control

Inject INJ PU into flowing cracks. Plug running holes with WP100 hydraulic cement. All active water ingress must stop before coating.

⏱ Full day depending on severity
Day 2

Surface Preparation

Hack and clean all surfaces. Cut V-grooves at wall-floor junction. Remove efflorescence.

⏱ Full day · Damp (not wet) surface required
Day 2–3

SBR-50 Primer + CM300 Coat 1

Apply SBR-50 while tacky, apply first CM300 coat. Extra 3 coats at wall-floor junction — 300mm each side.

⏱ 4–6 hrs · Cure: 24 hrs · Keep damp
Day 3–4

CM300 — Second Coat

Apply second coat perpendicular to first. Inspect for pinholes — apply extra brushed coat on any voids.

⏱ 4–6 hrs · Moist cure 3 days
Day 4–7

Moist Curing

Spray lightly with water 2–3 times daily. Cementitious systems shrink and crack if dried too fast.

⏱ 10 mins/day · Don't skip
Day 7+

✓ Inspect and Hand Over

Inspect all surfaces. System complete — basement can receive screed, tiles, or fit-out.

Total: 7 days start to finish
FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions.

Interior vs exterior — which should I choose for my existing basement?
For existing structures without excavation access: interior tanking with CM300. It's rated for negative hydrostatic pressure. Exterior is only practical for new construction or full renovation with excavation access.
My basement has water actively coming through the floor. Where do I start?
Start by stopping active water flow — FASTFIX WP100 hydraulic cement for holes and cracks, INJ PU injection for joints. Only when water flow is stopped can you apply CM300.
Can I tile over CM300 in the basement?
Yes. After 7 days full cure, CM300 is tile-ready. Use standard tile adhesive. Do not tile before 7 days — premature tiling causes adhesive failure.
Will CM300 work with a very high water table?
CM300 is rated for up to 3 bar negative hydrostatic pressure — equivalent to 30 metres of water head. Most residential and commercial basements are well within this range.
