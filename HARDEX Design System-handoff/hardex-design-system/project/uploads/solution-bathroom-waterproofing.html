The Problem: Why Bathroom Walls Stay Damp

Your Neighbour's Wall Is Wet Because of Your Bathroom

Water seeps through grout, through tiles, through the slab. The bedroom next door gets damp patches. The paint peels. Mould grows. The problem isn't the tiles — it's what's behind them.

Root Causes

  • No waterproofing layer under tiles — Most builders skip it. Tiles alone are not waterproof.
  • Failed grout joints — Grout is porous. Water passes through freely, especially at floor-wall junctions.
  • Structural movement cracks — Buildings settle. Rigid coatings crack and fail. Flexible systems stretch.
  • Wet area without proper slope — Standing water under tiles accelerates seepage.

Regrouting and waterproof paints are cosmetic fixes. They last 12–18 months. The system fix lasts 10+ years.

Detailed Application Guide

Preparation — The Step Contractors Skip

  1. Hack the surface — Create a rough texture for CM200 to bond. Use a chisel or angle grinder.
  2. Fill cracks — Use FASTFIX crack filler on any cracks >0.5 mm. Allow 4–6 hrs.
  3. Wet the surface — Substrate should be damp (not dripping) before primer. This improves adhesion.
  4. Junction prep — Floor-wall junctions are highest failure points. Extra preparation here is critical.

SBR-50 Primer Application

  1. Dilute SBR-50 with water (1:1 ratio). Apply by brush to entire surface.
  2. Do NOT wait for full dry — apply CM200 when primer is tacky (2–4 hrs).
  3. If primer dries completely, reapply before CM200 coat.

CM200 Application — Critical Points

  1. Mix powder with clean water per TDS ratio. Mix to lump-free paste.
  2. Apply first coat by brush — working into corners, pipe penetrations, drains.
  3. Apply two extra coats at floor-wall junctions — this is where 80% of leaks start.
  4. Second coat perpendicular to first — fills pinholes left by first coat.
  5. Do not rush tiling — 7 days cure is minimum. Premature tiling causes delamination.

  • Waterproof paint — Fine for walls above tile line. Fails under water pressure at floor level.

  • CM200 — Applied under tiles. Bonds to substrate. Bridges cracks. The only permanent fix.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply CM200 over existing tiles without hacking?

Not recommended. CM200 needs a rough, absorptive substrate to bond. Glazed tiles have zero absorption. Hack and prepare properly for a permanent result.

My bathroom is already built and leaking. What do I do?

Option A: Hack tiles, apply CM200, re-tile (permanent fix). Option B: If hacking isn't possible, apply HARDPROOF CM200 to the exterior wall where seepage appears — limits damage but doesn't fix source.

How do I waterproof around pipes and drains?

Apply three extra coats of CM200 around pipe penetrations using a brush. Fill the gap between pipe and floor/wall with HARDPROOF PU350 sealant before CM200 application.

What tiles can go over CM200?

Any ceramic, vitrified, or natural stone tile. Use standard tile adhesive. CM200 surface is compatible with all standard adhesives and grouts.

CM200 vs CM300 — which for my bathroom?

CM200 is correct for bathrooms, balconies, and wet areas. CM300 is a heavier two-component system for basements and foundations under hydrostatic pressure. Overkill for bathrooms and more expensive.

How much CM200 do I need for a standard bathroom?

A 10 m² bathroom (floor + 1.5 m wall height) = ~35 m² total surface. At 1.5–2.0 kg/m², you need 55–70 kg of CM200. Buy 3–4 bags of 20 kg.
